        What to choose

        • Wheels at the back, with a ramped start. Climbing and accelerating press trailing wheels down and unload leading ones. A hard start is what lifts the support, so ramp the drive up over about a second.
        • Fit a ring or a band to a printed wheel. A bare printed wheel slips on a smooth floor.
        • Print the fit pieces first and measure the TT motor's holes and the servo's case against them before printing a cradle.
        • Keep fingers clear of the wheels and try each program first with the wheels off the floor.

          Wheels: LEGO, stock TT, printed with a ring, printed with a TPU tyre

          The default printed wheel is one piece with a groove for a standard 3 mm O-ring (38 x 3, 50 x 3 or 58 x 3) or a rubber band; a hub with a separate TPU 95A tyre is the alternative. Friction used: rubber and O-ring 0.8, TPU 0.7, stock TT tyre 0.6, bare plastic 0.3. The same robot on each, skid, wheels at the back:

          Servo or TT motor

          For the controllers without LEGO motors. The 360-degree servo has twice the torque (0.157 N m against 0.078) and needs only a pulse pin per wheel. The yellow TT motor is more than twice as fast (200 rpm against 83), needs an H-bridge board (TB6612FNG or DRV8833; an L298N loses about 2 V) and draws up to 1.5 A each at stall. Both run open loop: equal commands do not give a straight line. Neither motor limits the slope: grip and tipping do.
