ARDUINO UNO Q / TINKERKIT BRACCIO SERVOS

A printed biped, from simulation to the bench

Bench prototype

Status: verified in simulation only

This robot has never been assembled or powered. The parts have not been printed as a set, no servo has been fitted, and the firmware has not been built or flashed. Everything on this page comes from the mesh files, the makers' published servo and board data, and a MuJoCo model. Dimensions the makers do not publish are assumed and listed.

01

What it is

A Microduck-style biped rebuilt around the six servos of a stock TinkerKit Braccio kit and an Arduino UNO Q. Four SR431 servos sit at the hips: two on the torso for hip roll, one on each yoke for hip pitch. Two SR311 servos at the far end of each thigh turn the knees. The shin carries a fixed foot; there are no ankles. A camera board stands in a mount at the front edge of the deck, looking the way the toes point. The printable parts of the biped are in stl/, listed with sizes and checksums in the manifest; none has been printed. The UNO Q and the Braccio shield are strapped to a deck on the torso. Servos are held by cable ties.

Seven printed parts, three fit-test pieces, six actuated joints: left and right hip roll, hip pitch and knee pitch.

02

Digital assembly test

Full report

The seven STLs and a parametric model of the servos are placed by the joint chain and checked with exact mesh booleans: interference at the neutral pose and through each joint's sweep, servo fit in the cradles, horn screw access, board clearance, mass, centre of mass and static balance.

        05

        From simulation to the robot

        Details

        The simulator and the board take the same command lines: six joint angles in degrees, in the robot's own convention. A calibration table maps each joint to a shield channel, a zero and a direction. The same trajectory file plays on either.
