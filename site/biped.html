01

What it is

A Microduck-style biped rebuilt around the six servos of a stock TinkerKit Braccio kit and an Arduino UNO Q. Four SR431 servos sit at the hips: two on the torso for hip roll, one on each yoke for hip pitch. Two SR311 servos at the far end of each thigh turn the knees. The shin carries a fixed foot; there are no ankles. A camera board stands in a mount at the front edge of the deck, looking the way the toes point. The printable parts of the biped are in stl/, listed with sizes and checksums in the manifest. The UNO Q and the Braccio shield are strapped to a deck on the torso. Servos are held by cable ties.

Seven printed parts, three fit-test pieces, six actuated joints: left and right hip roll, hip pitch and knee pitch.

Digital assembly test

Full report

The seven STLs and a parametric model of the servos are placed by the joint chain and checked with exact mesh booleans: interference at the neutral pose and through each joint's sweep, servo fit in the cradles, horn screw access, board clearance, mass, centre of mass and static balance.

        04b

        Walk, turn, jump, sit

        Motion study

        The same robot in a fuller servo model (dead band, gear play, compliance, command delay, the 4 A supply), with the gait's parameters found by a seeded search. On the comb soles it steps: 44.4 mm/s, a sole 3.85 mm clear of the floor, peak torque 0.647 of the limit, upright in all 30 runs over floor friction 0.3 to 1.0 and mass 0.9 to 1.1 times. With the flat soles first drawn the same search finds no gait that lifts a foot. The heading drifts about 0.81 deg/s and a step difference between the legs does not steer it reliably, so the control page offers forward only.

        Sitting is a squat to the hip pitch limit. With the pulses stopped the robot sinks on to the servo travel stops and stays on its soles; enabled again it stands up. The jump is a hop of 0.06 s and stays locked: it runs four servos at stall torque from a supply that cannot feed them and can strip gears or break parts. The control core refuses a command that would swing one comb sole into the other.

        05

        From simulation to the robot

        Details

        The simulator and the board take the same command lines: six joint angles in degrees, in the robot's own convention. A calibration table maps each joint to a shield channel, a zero and a direction. The same trajectory file plays on either.

        06

        The Microduck-look biped

        Full report

        A second biped that keeps the Microduck's own shape: its body, head, thigh covers and feet at 1.25 times the size, on the servos of two TinkerKit Braccio kits. Fifteen joints: hip yaw, hip roll, hip pitch, knee and ankle on each leg, a pitching neck, and a head that pitches, turns, rolls and opens its beak. Two Arduino UNO Q boards with their shields ride on its back; a camera board sits behind the eye.

        It comes with two sets of feet: the Microduck's soles widened towards the centre line, or comb feet whose fingers cross the centre line between the other foot's, so either foot alone is under the body. Build this one: the configurator lists its files, parts and wiring.
