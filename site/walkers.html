01

Three builds, one mechanism

Full report

On each side three axles sit 16 mm apart with a 16-tooth gear on each; the middle gear idles. A 12 mm crank on each outer axle carries the leg on two pins 32 mm apart, so the leg does not rotate: the flat foot stays level and moves on a circle. The legs are half a turn apart and one motor drives each side. One crank turn moves the robot 48 mm. The same chassis on wheels is on the two-wheeled page.

03

Assembly and balance

  • Print the fit coupon first: a cross axle in the keyed hole, a pin in the 4.95 mm hole, an axle turning freely in the 5.35 mm hole. Millimetres, 100 percent scale.
  • Point the motors backward. Pointing forward they move the centre of mass toward the front edge of the feet.
  • Use a half bush on the outboard end of each idler axle. The cranks sweep past it and a full bush leaves 0.2 mm.
  • Keep the controller centred side to side. Each foot reaches only 10.5 mm past the centreline.
  • The walkers cannot turn. A leg on parallel cranks only moves the body straight forward or back.
  • Stay at or below 90 crank rpm. Faster, the feet leave the floor and the heading wanders.
  • Keep fingers and cables clear of the cranks and gears and turn each side by hand through a full turn before powering it.

    Balance through the cycle

    The lower foot carries the robot. It is balanced while the centre of mass is over that foot's outline; the margin is the distance to the nearest edge.

      05

      Parts and programs

      Folder

      Each build's files to print, the other parts, and programs to load. For the LEGO controllers the first route is Mindstorms Robot Creator: paste the program into its editor under "Custom (edit below)". It runs the program on a 51515 hub with Pybricks firmware over Bluetooth, saves a project for the LEGO app for stock firmware, and saves a file for the EV3 to copy to the brick.

      06

      Camera, phase and power

      • EV3 phase: the EV3 motor encoders count from power-up, so set the legs by hand at the start: left foot down, right foot up, then press the centre button. Mark the cranks.
      • 51515 phase: these motors have absolute encoders, so the programs home themselves once the two zero angles are measured and entered.
      • Pixy2 with the pan-tilt kit: print camera_head_pantilt in place of camera_head and measure the kit's base plate against the 72 x 56 mm platform. A standard Pixy2 needs the LEGO firmware and the EV3 cable to work with the EV3 blocks. The pan-tilt servos are driven by the Pixy2 itself.
      • 51515 hub camera: the Pixy2 cannot plug into the hub's ports. The head is a plate with seven pin holes for a self-contained Wi-Fi camera or a LEGO sensor.
      • Pi power: the Build HAT's 8 V adapter means a tether; a 7.2 to 8.5 V battery on its barrel jack frees the robot.
