Analysed and simulated

Status: analysed and simulated only, never assembled

Nothing on this page has been printed, assembled or powered. No controller has run on hardware. The numbers come from the printable files, the makers' published data and a MuJoCo model. Masses and dimensions the makers do not publish are assumed and listed in the report.

01

The two builds

Full report

One mechanism, two sets of electronics. On each side three axles sit 16 mm apart with a 16-tooth gear on each; the middle gear idles. A 12 mm crank on each outer axle carries the leg on two pins 32 mm apart, so the leg does not rotate: the flat foot stays level and moves on a circle. The legs are half a turn apart and one motor drives each side. One crank turn moves the robot 48 mm.

Assembly and balance

Printed parts, axles, gears, cranks, motors, controller and camera placed as the design's assembly steps say, then checked with exact mesh intersections at rest and at 72 crank phases. Where the steps leave a placement open, one was chosen and is stated in the report.

    Balance through the cycle

    The lower foot carries the robot. It is balanced while the centre of mass is over that foot's outline; the margin is the distance to the nearest edge.

        Servo or TT motor

        For the bases without LEGO motors. The 360-degree servo has twice the torque (0.157 N m against 0.078) and needs only a pulse pin per wheel. The yellow TT motor is more than twice as fast (200 rpm against 83), needs an H-bridge board (TB6612FNG or DRV8833; an L298N loses about 2 V) and draws up to 1.5 A each at stall. Both run open loop. Neither motor limits the slope: grip and tipping do.

        07

        Controllers and camera

        • EV3: a Pybricks sketch, large motors on ports B and C. The EV3 encoders are relative, so the legs are set by hand once (left foot down, right foot up) and the encoders hold the phase from there.
        • Raspberry Pi: a buildhat sketch, 51515 motors on Build HAT ports A and B. Their absolute encoders let the program home itself.
        • Wheeled builds: two-speed drive sketches for the EV3, the 51515 hub, the Build HAT and a plain Raspberry Pi with servos or a TB6612FNG. None for the UNO Q or BaseX yet.
        • No sketch has run on hardware. All are in walkers/controllers/ and are only syntax-checked.
        • Pixy2: a standard Pixy2 is the same board as the LEGO edition and needs the LEGO firmware and the EV3 cable for the EV3 blocks. Its hardware bag has no LEGO brackets. The pan-tilt servos are driven by the Pixy2; moving them from the EV3 is not supported by the LEGO firmware and is untested with the stock one.
        • Power: the Pi build is designed around the Build HAT's 8 V mains adapter, so it walks on a tether unless a 7.2 to 8.5 V battery is added.
